\subsection{Train run 5 (set baseline: original RND)}
\label{sec:train-run-5}

\paragraph{Run.}
Train run~5 fixes a clean baseline: three RND configurations, each on $300$ fresh seeds
(\texttt{a\_\allowbreak seed} $600$--$899$, disjoint from the $0$--$599$ used by every prior run),
same SAC / \texttt{PointMaze\_\allowbreak Large-v3} stack and training-episode metric as run~3.2.4.
The three arms are:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{original-small} --- the \emph{original} RND recipe \citep{burda2019exploration}
  transplanted onto this task on the train-run-3 RND network size, swept over eight intrinsic
  weights that race under a pruning controller;
\item \textbf{C2} --- the run-3.2.2 RND benchmark (Adam, $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{mse}}$, $\beta{=}100$,
  no reward normalization), re-run at $300$ seeds; and
\item \textbf{N1} --- the run-3.2.3 reward-normalized winner (Adam, $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{mse}}$,
  $\beta{=}10^{4}$, reward normalization $\gamma_{\mathrm{norm}}{=}0.99$), re-run at $300$ seeds.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Purpose.}
Two goals. First, the project has always compared its bonuses against its own ``RND benchmark''
(the C2 line), never against a faithful \emph{original} RND; original-small is that reference,
assembling the scattered original-RND facts of Section~\ref{sec:rnd-norm} and
Table~\ref{tab:rnd-norm} / Table~\ref{tab:trainrun3-2-2-rnd-init} into one run. Second, C2 and N1
already have fresh-seed data (runs 3.2.2/3.2.3/3.2.4); re-running them at $300$ seeds lets us test,
per previous run, whether the new data differs enough to stand alone or whether the old data may be
pooled in --- the substitution check under \emph{Results}.

\paragraph{Original RND, component by component.}
Before the translation we restate the original recipe one component at a time, each time giving the
original \citep{burda2019exploration} value, then CleanRL's \citep{huang2022cleanrl}, then --- where
it is informative --- ExPLORe's \citep{li2023accelerating} state-input RND. The two Atari references
run no continuous-control task (verified: the original repository and paper evaluate only Atari plus
an MNIST toy, and CleanRL's only RND is the Atari envpool script), so ExPLORe supplies the
state/continuous reference. The full cell-by-cell values are in Table~\ref{tab:trainrun5-hparams}.

\paragraph{State normalization.}
The original whitens the RND input by a running mean/std with a clip to $[-5,5]$ (no denominator
$\epsilon$), and seeds those statistics from a random agent before training: $128$ parallel copies
$\times\,50$ steps $=6400$ warm-up transitions. CleanRL is identical except it excludes the initial
reset observations from the warm-up. ExPLORe applies \emph{no} state normalization at all --- the
predictor and target consume the raw state-action vector.

\paragraph{Intrinsic reward normalization.}
Define $r^{\mathrm{int}}_t$ as the raw bonus on the transition collected at step $t$,
$\gamma_{\mathrm{norm}}$ as the intrinsic discount ($=0.99$), $F_t$ as the forward-filtered
intrinsic return (one accumulator per environment copy, not reset at episode boundaries;
$F_0=r^{\mathrm{int}}_0$),
and $\sigma_F$ as the running standard deviation of all filter values so far. The original filter
and normalized reward are
\begin{align}
F_t = \gamma_{\mathrm{norm}}\,F_{t-1} + r^{\mathrm{int}}_t,
\qquad
\hat{r}^{\mathrm{int}}_t = r^{\mathrm{int}}_t / \sigma_F ,
\end{align}
with no mean subtracted, so the bonus stays non-negative. CleanRL implements the same filter and
running-std division with $\gamma_{\mathrm{norm}}{=}0.99$. ExPLORe uses \emph{no} reward
normalization --- its bonus is the raw distillation error times a constant coefficient
($\mathrm{coeff}{=}1.0$).

\paragraph{Target and predictor architecture.}
The original target is a three-layer convolutional trunk (LeakyReLU) followed by a single linear
projection to $m{=}512$; the predictor is a \emph{separate} network of the same trunk architecture
(independently initialized, no weight sharing) that adds \emph{two} extra $512$-wide ReLU layers
before its $512$-output --- the predictor is deliberately \emph{deeper} than the target.
Both use orthogonal initialization with gain $\sqrt2$ and zero biases. CleanRL matches this shape
(its convolutional LeakyReLU slope is the PyTorch default $0.01$ rather than the original's $0.2$).
ExPLORe departs sharply: a state-action MLP with hidden widths $(256,256,256)$ and a $256$-feature
head, Xavier/Glorot initialization, and predictor \emph{identical} to the target (symmetric, no
extra depth).

\paragraph{Bonus readout.}
Let $e\in\mathbb{R}^m$ be the per-dimension error between target and predictor features. The
original reads out the bonus as the \emph{mean} over dimensions,
$\tfrac1m\sum_j e_j^2$ (no $\tfrac12$ factor), and trains the predictor with the same reduction.
CleanRL instead reports the bonus as $\tfrac12\sum_j e_j^2$ (a sum, halved) while --- inconsistently
--- taking the mean over dimensions in the predictor \emph{loss}. ExPLORe uses the same
mean-over-dimensions readout as the original paper (times its constant coefficient).

\paragraph{Predictor optimizer and update proportion.}
The original trains the predictor with Adam at learning rate $10^{-4}$ (betas $(0.9,0.999)$,
$\epsilon{=}10^{-8}$), inside one joint optimizer that also carries the policy and value networks,
with no gradient clipping. CleanRL is the same except $\epsilon{=}10^{-5}$ and a global-norm
gradient clip of $0.5$. On the fraction of each batch used to train the predictor there is a
documentation-vs-code split worth stating plainly: the RND paper's Table~5 lists an update
proportion of $0.25$, and CleanRL uses $0.25$, but the \emph{original repository's own code}
defaults to $1.0$ (train on the whole batch). ExPLORe takes one Adam step ($3\times10^{-4}$) per
environment step on a single fresh transition, with no subsampling.

\paragraph{The RL side.}
The original and CleanRL are PPO with \emph{two} value heads: an intrinsic head trained on
non-episodic returns (intrinsic discount $\gamma_{\mathrm{int}}{=}0.99$) and an extrinsic head on
episodic returns (extrinsic discount $\gamma_{\mathrm{ext}}$; the original code uses $0.99$, its
paper and CleanRL use $0.999$). The two advantages are combined with an extrinsic coefficient of
$2$ and an intrinsic coefficient of $1$ --- a $2\!:\!1$ extrinsic-to-intrinsic weighting, run with
$128$ parallel environments. ExPLORe instead runs RLPD (SAC with a $50/50$ offline/online replay
mix, UTD $20$, one environment) and \emph{adds} the bonus directly to the scalar reward with no
separate value head. The project's own RND (train run 3) and run~5 likewise use SAC with a single
reward stream $r^{\mathrm{ext}}+\beta\,r^{\mathrm{int}}$.

\paragraph{From Atari-PPO to PointMaze-SAC: the translation choices.}
original-small keeps the original RND settings above wherever the SAC/PointMaze/state setting allows,
and makes the following explicit translations:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{CNN $\to$ MLP.} The $84\times84$ convolutional trunk becomes a fully-connected trunk
  on the $4$-dimensional state $(x,y,v_x,v_y)$.
\item \textbf{Small widths, deeper predictor.} original-small takes the train-run-3 RND \emph{widths}
  ($256$ hidden, output $m{=}128$) but keeps the original's defining \emph{asymmetry}: the predictor
  is one $128$-wide LeakyReLU$(0.2)$ block deeper than the target. Target
  $=\;$state$(4)\!\to\!256\!\to\!\mathrm{LReLU}\!\to\!128$; predictor
  $=\;$state$(4)\!\to\!256\!\to\!\mathrm{LReLU}\!\to\!128\!\to\!\mathrm{LReLU}\!\to\!128$ (output
  dim stays $128$ so the distillation error dimension matches the target).
\item \textbf{Activation.} LeakyReLU with negative slope $0.2$ --- the original's
  \texttt{tf.nn.\allowbreak leaky\_\allowbreak relu} default, \emph{not} PyTorch's $0.01$ (which is
  what CleanRL inherits).
\item \textbf{Readout.} the original mean-over-dimensions $\tfrac1m\sum_j e_j^2$
  (\texttt{mse\_\allowbreak mean}).
\item \textbf{Optimizer.} Adam at $10^{-4}$, $\epsilon{=}10^{-8}$ (the original's value).
\item \textbf{Update proportion $1.0$.} following the original \emph{repository code} default (train
  on the whole replay batch), not the paper's Table-5 value of $0.25$.
\item \textbf{Warm-up.} $6400$ random-agent environment steps (the original's count), rolled on a
  fresh throwaway environment so the training visit counts are untouched.
\item \textbf{Reward normalization.} on, $\gamma_{\mathrm{norm}}{=}0.99$.
\item \textbf{Single reward stream.} SAC forms $r^{\mathrm{ext}}+\beta\,r^{\mathrm{int}}$ in the
  replay buffer; there is no separate intrinsic value head, so the $2\!:\!1$ advantage weighting
  becomes a single scalar $\beta$. The point $\beta{=}0.5$ is the faithful counterpart of the
  original's $2\!:\!1$ extrinsic-to-intrinsic ratio.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Beta sweep and pruning.}
original-small sweeps $\beta\in\{10^{-2},10^{-1},0.5,1,10,10^{2},10^{3},10^{4}\}$. The eight betas
race: let $\bar R_\beta$, $s_\beta$, and $n_\beta$ be the mean, sample standard deviation, and count
of a beta's finished-seed final training-episode rewards, and let the \emph{bar} be the largest
$\bar R_\beta$ over betas with $n_\beta\ge 30$. A beta is stopped once
\begin{align}
n_\beta \ge 30
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\bar R_\beta + 2.576\,\frac{s_\beta}{\sqrt{n_\beta}} \;<\; \mathrm{bar}
\end{align}
(its one-sided $99\%$ upper confidence limit sits below the running best mean); survivors run to the
full $300$ seeds. C2 and N1 never prune and run all $300$ seeds.

\paragraph{Pipeline.}
Figure~\ref{fig:trainrun5-pipeline} traces the original RND data flow --- environment step, next
state, observation whitening, frozen target and trainable predictor, per-dimension error, readout,
forward-filter reward normalization, $\beta$-scaling, and the sum with the extrinsic reward feeding
the RL update --- with CleanRL's departures marked as callouts: its predictor \emph{update} feeds
the stored current-step observation rather than the next observation; its convolutional LeakyReLU
slope is $0.01$; it clips gradients at global norm $0.5$; and it normalizes advantages per
minibatch. original-small follows the original everywhere the figure's solid path runs (next-state
input throughout, no gradient clip), on SAC's single reward stream.
