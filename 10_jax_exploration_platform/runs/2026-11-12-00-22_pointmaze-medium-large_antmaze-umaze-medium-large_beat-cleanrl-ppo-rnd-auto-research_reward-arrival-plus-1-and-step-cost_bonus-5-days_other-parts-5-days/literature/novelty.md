# Closest published work to the held divisor and to the fast last layer inside the rollout

Both ideas change one part of the same baseline: the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) agent with Random Network Distillation (RND) of CleanRL (`ppo_rnd_envpool.py`), which follows Burda, Edwards, Storkey and Klimov (2019).

## 1. Held divisor

### The idea

In the baseline, the intrinsic reward is the squared error of the trained predictor network against the fixed random target network on the next observation, divided by a running estimate of the standard deviation of the discounted sum of intrinsic rewards. This divisor is updated at every rollout for the whole run. With the held divisor, the divisor is updated in the same way for a fixed fraction of the planned training steps (1/16; 1/64 and 1/4 were also tried) and then keeps the value it has at that moment until the end of the run. Everything else is unchanged. In the baseline the divisor shrinks together with the prediction error, so the learner receives an intrinsic reward of about the same size during the whole run. With the divisor held, the intrinsic reward after the hold is the prediction error times a constant, so it decreases as the predictor learns, and the policy then optimises the extrinsic reward. The study measured, on PointMaze with 256 seeds, a whole-curve mean of the training extrinsic return 0.004 to 0.011 above tuned RND in four cases.

### Verdict

Close. No published paper was found that proposes or evaluates stopping the updates of RND's reward divisor part-way through training. Three parts of the idea are already public:

1. **The observation.** Schwarke et al. (2023) state that RND's running reward normalisation keeps the intrinsic reward from decreasing towards zero, and that the decrease allows optimisation toward the task reward.
2. **The reward after the hold.** After the hold, the intrinsic reward is the prediction error times a constant. That is RND without reward normalisation and with a fixed weight, which is what Schwarke et al. use.
3. **The mechanism, in code.** The RND reward normaliser of the RSL-RL library stops updating after 100 million samples when reward normalisation is switched on.

The difference in one sentence: the held divisor keeps RND's running divisor for a set fraction of the run (1/16) and then holds it, inside CleanRL's PPO with RND (squared error, two value heads, non-episodic intrinsic returns), so the fixed scale is taken from the early running estimate and the effect of the hold is measured; Schwarke et al. remove the divisor and set the weight themselves, and the stop in the RSL-RL code is a fixed sample count that no paper found describes or evaluates.

What the sources support:

1. Supported: the held divisor is not one of the published variants the study names (Never Give Up, NovelD, Distributional Random Network Distillation, coin-flip networks, Exploration via Elliptical Episodic Bonuses, random distribution distillation, self-supervised network distillation).
2. Supported: no opened paper reports results for a divisor that is updated for a fraction of the run and then held.
3. Not supported: that the observation about the normaliser is new (Schwarke et al., 2023).
4. Not supported: that an RND reward which decreases during training because it is not re-normalised is new (Schwarke et al., 2023).
5. Not supported: that stopping the updates of a reward normaliser is a new mechanism (RSL-RL and PFRL code).

## 2. Fast last layer inside the rollout

### The idea

In the baseline, the RND predictor is trained only in the optimisation epochs after each rollout, so the bonus at a state does not change during the rollout in which the state is visited. With the fast last layer, the predictor's final linear layer alone takes one gradient step (step size 0.1) per environment step during the rollout, on the transition just collected. The usual epoch updates of the whole predictor stay. The bonus at a state therefore decreases immediately after the state is visited. The study measured, on PointMaze Large, a first arrival at the goal in about half the environment steps of tuned RND.

### Verdict

Close. No published work was found in which the last layer of an RND predictor takes a gradient step at every environment step during an on-policy rollout, in addition to the usual epoch updates. Updating a novelty estimate at every environment step, so that the bonus of a state decreases right after a visit, is published in several forms:

1. a closed-form update, at every step, of an inverse covariance matrix over learned features, including a version that is not reset between episodes (Henaff et al., 2022);
2. one gradient step of a density model on each state as it is seen (Ostrovski et al., 2017);
3. one optimiser step of the whole RND predictor on each input as it is seen, in a grid world with a table-based agent (the RND comparison method of Bhaskara and Wang, 2026);
4. one update of a novelty network per time step on replayed states (Lobel et al., 2023);
5. two update rates, the linear last layer at every step and the network below it less often, in contextual bandits (Riquelme et al., 2018; Xu et al., 2022).

The difference in one sentence: in the fast last layer, only the final linear layer of the RND predictor takes one gradient step on each transition as it is collected during a PPO rollout, while the whole predictor is still trained in the usual epochs after the rollout; the closest works either update a different estimator at every step (an inverse covariance matrix, a density model, a count network on replayed states) or update the whole RND predictor at every step as its only training in a small table-based test.

What the sources support:

1. Supported: this arrangement (last layer only, one gradient step per environment step on the transition just collected, inside a PPO rollout, epoch updates kept) was not found in any opened source.
2. Supported: it is not one of the published variants the study names.
3. Not supported: that updating a novelty estimate at every environment step is new.
4. Not supported: that a bonus which decreases inside the episode or the rollout is new.
5. Not supported: that training an RND predictor at every environment step is new (Bhaskara and Wang, 2026).
6. A linear layer trained on fixed features to predict a random target estimates the quantity that elliptical bonuses compute in closed form: Ash et al. (2022) show that linear regressors trained on random noise approximate the elliptical bonus. The fast last layer can therefore be described as a gradient-step version of the non-episodic elliptical bonus of Henaff et al. (2022), computed on the RND predictor's own features. No opened source states this relation for RND; it is an inference from Ash et al.
