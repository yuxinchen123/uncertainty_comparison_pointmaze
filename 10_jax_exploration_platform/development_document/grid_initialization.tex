\section{Uneven initial bonuses in deterministic grid exploration}
\label{sec:grid-initialization}

\subsection{Question and measurements}
Uniform initial bonuses and exact visit-count decay provide the reference condition.
The experiments test whether changing only the initial bonus field delays goal discovery,
and whether untrained coordinate networks produce such fields on a snake-shaped path.
Tabular policies and neural policies are evaluated separately.

Let $s$ denote a traversable cell, $c(s)$ its fixed initial bonus, and $n$ its arrival count
after the current transition. Let $w(s)$ be the raw initial field and $\mathcal S$ the set of
traversable cells. Positive fields have a common initial mean and an identical decay rule:
\begin{align}
c(s) &= \frac{w(s)}{|\mathcal S|^{-1}\sum_{u\in\mathcal S}w(u)}, &
b(s,n) &= \frac{c(s)}{\sqrt n}.
\end{align}
The no-intrinsic-bonus comparison sets the bonus to zero. Counts persist across episode
resets and are updated on the reached cell, including blocked moves and goal arrivals.
Automatic resets do not add a second arrival reward.

Let $T_i$ be the first-goal time in training transitions for policy copy $i$, $B$ its
transition budget, and $K$ the number of completed copies. For a copy that never reaches the
goal, take $T_i>B$. The primary metric is the mean capped first-goal time:
\begin{equation}
M=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\min(T_i,B).
\end{equation}
Lower values are better. The goal-found percentage includes every copy; unsuccessful copies
are never dropped from the primary mean. Coverage is the fraction of traversable cells visited.
Reported intervals resample independent random-field seeds as groups, retaining the policy
seeds belonging to each field. Deterministic fields resample policy seeds instead.
These measurements establish behavior within a finite budget, rather than permanent trapping.

\subsection{Fixed CleanRL PPO-RND settings: normalization and learned decay}
\label{sec:grid-fixed-settings}
This study fixes PPO at CleanRL PPO-RND's scalar settings and tunes only the learning rate,
the entropy coefficient and the intrinsic weight $\beta$, which multiplies the intrinsic
advantage. It adds the two CleanRL normalizations and an online RND whose bonus decays as its
predictor learns. The maps are the straight grid of 32 cells and the snake grid of width 16
with two turns (50 cells), each with the tabular and the neural policy.

Recall $c(s)$ (the mean-one initial field) and $n$ (the arrivals at $s$) from
Section~\ref{sec:grid-initialization}. Define $g$ as a frozen random target network, $f_t$ as a
predictor after $t$ updates, both evaluated on the cell's input $\phi(s)$, and
$e_t(s)=\tfrac12\lVert g(\phi(s))-f_t(\phi(s))\rVert^2$ as the online RND error. The two decay
rules are
\begin{align}
b_{\mathrm{count}}(s,n) &= \frac{c(s)}{\sqrt n}, &
b_{\mathrm{online}}(s,t) &= e_t(s).
\end{align}
The online bonus is CleanRL's raw error and is not scaled to mean one. With state
normalization, $\phi(s)$ is the cell's coordinates whitened by running statistics and clipped to
$[-5,5]$; the statistics start from 50 random-action rollouts of 128 steps. Reward normalization
divides every bonus by the running standard deviation of its discounted sum.

\subsection{Earlier study: behavior and relation to previous work}
Initial-field maps, total-visitation maps, and late-visitation maps use shared color scales
within each comparison. Training-position samples and farthest-discovered distance distinguish
continued progress from repeatedly visiting the same part of a path. Action arrows show legal
action probabilities; cell colors separately show the probability of blocked moves.
The representative seed is the one closest to median capped first-goal time, with increasing
field seed and policy seed as fixed tie breaks. Every seed remains in the numerical results.

Visitation maps and policy arrows follow the presentation in
\href{https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.00119}{Long-Term Visitation Value for Deep Exploration in
Sparse-Reward Reinforcement Learning}, Figures~3 and~19.
Trajectory and coverage comparisons follow
\href{https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/9c7900fac04a701cbed83256b76dbaa3-Paper-Conference.pdf}{Maximum
State Entropy Exploration using Predecessor and Successor Representations}, Figures~1 and~3.
Uneven initial random-network errors were identified in
\href{https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/yang24w.html}{Exploration and Anti-Exploration with
Distributional Random Network Distillation}. The present experiment holds the count-decay
rule exact and tests the consequences of the initial field in isolation.
The inverse-square-root rule is a reference condition here; no universal optimality claim
is made for every deterministic environment or policy optimizer.
